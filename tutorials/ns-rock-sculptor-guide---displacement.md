## Structured Notes

> **Third-party add-on note:** This tutorial covers the **Displacement** tab of **NS Rock Sculptor**, a paid third-party Blender add-on by Nick Sayce (NS). It exposes two independent displacement paths — a material-based displacement (masked via a Colour Ramp) and a stock Blender **Displace modifier** ("Rock Displace" in the modifier stack) driven by a procedural texture — both add-on-configured, not raw Blender defaults. Do not confuse the add-on's own "Displacement Mask Colour Ramp" tooling with a from-scratch manual Blender displacement setup.

### Core Technique
Two complementary displacement paths on the same rock: (1) material/shader-based displacement gated by a Colour Ramp mask (built from a noise texture) that controls where the crack/detail pattern shows through vs. stays smooth, and (2) a genuine Blender **Displace modifier** ("Rock Displace") driven by a procedural texture (Clouds, size ~0.5) with its own paintable Vertex Group mask ("Rock Displace Mask") for fully manual, hand-painted control of where geometry-level displacement appears.

### Summary
The add-on's Subdivision Surface is added first (author's recommendation: viewport level 4 is "absolutely fine", 5 is "great" but heavier) since it directly affects displacement/edge-wear quality. Frame 000 shows the base rock with material displacement at strength 1.5 and the Subdivision modifier (Catmull-Clark, viewport level 4, Optimal Display) plus the "Rock Displace" modifier both visible in the stack. Frame 001 shows the Displacement Mask Colour Ramp being edited — dragging the ramp stops close together abruptly truncates a visible crack instead of letting it run the full length of the rock, giving more natural, "bitty" broken detail. Frame 002 shows the same rock after the ramp stops were spread out again for a smoother transition. Frame 003/004 show the **Displace modifier** panel itself (Texture: Clouds, Direction: Normal, Strength, Midlevel, Vertex Group: "Rock Displace Mask", "Paint Displace Mask" button) producing a visible wave/marble-like ripple pattern across the rock surface. Frame 005 shows Weight Paint mode on the "Rock Displace Mask" vertex group — a rainbow weight gradient (blue = 0 low weight through red = 1 full weight) painted directly onto the rock to hand-control exactly where the Displace-modifier bump reads as strong vs. suppressed.

### Key Steps
1. Add the add-on's Subdivision Surface first — viewport level 4 is generally sufficient (5 looks even better but costs more); this level also affects how Edge Wear reads later.
2. **Material displacement:** enable it and push Strength up (e.g. 1.0 -> 1.5) to make it clearly visible.
3. Material displacement is masked by a **Displacement Mask Colour Ramp** (under the Colour Ramps tab) built on a noise texture — black areas show displacement, white hides it. Dragging the ramp's white stop fully right applies displacement to the whole rock; bringing the two ramp stops close together creates an abrupt cutoff (e.g. truncating a crack mid-way); spreading them out softens the transition.
4. Material displacement also has its own Scale and "displacement filter amount" controls, plus access to the Filters tab for further shaping.
5. Reason to combine mask displacement with material displacement rather than only cranking mask displacement: doing so lets you add material displacement independently on top of the masked pattern instead of only amplifying what the mask already reveals — keep material displacement subtle (author uses ~0.04) since it stacks on top of the mask result.
6. **Displace modifier ("Rock Displace"):** the second, independent displacement path — appears as a real modifier in Blender's Modifier stack. Uses a procedural texture (author swaps to Clouds, size 0.5, calling it "one of the most natural rocky bitty things you can do").
7. Author's guidance: you *can* run both material displacement and the Displace modifier together, but stacking too much of both starts producing messy, broken geometry artifacts — dial back Strength (down toward ~0.3) if you see that happening.
8. The Displace modifier has its own paintable mask: click "Paint Displaced Mask" to enter Weight Paint on the "Rock Displace Mask" vertex group. 100% white = fully visible everywhere by default; the author keeps an Invert-Weights quick-access item in his own quick menu for fast toggling.
9. Paint directly in Weight Paint mode (works fine even mid-edit-mode) to localize displacement — e.g. paint out (set to zero/invert to remove) a small area where the Displace modifier is visibly breaking the geometry, then Shift-drag to blur/feather the edge of the painted-out region for a clean transition. This is how you create the smooth, un-displaced surface patches seen on some real rocks/boulders.
10. If displacement strength is too high in a masked area, the geometry visibly mangles — reduce strength or clean up the mask rather than pushing through it.

### Nodes / Settings
- **NS Rock Sculptor Displacement tab:** Subdivision Surface add button (viewport level ~4 recommended), Material Displacement toggle + Strength + Scale + "displacement filter amount", Displacement Mask (built from the Colour Ramps tab's noise-texture-driven ramp).
- **Displace modifier ("Rock Displace", real Blender modifier):** Texture = Clouds (Size 0.5 used here, but freely swappable — "do what you want"), Direction = Normal, Strength, Midlevel, Vertex Group = "Rock Displace Mask", "Paint Displace Mask" quick-entry button.
- **Weight Paint workflow:** vertex group "Rock Displace Mask" painted in Weight Paint mode (rainbow gradient visualization — blue=low/no displacement, red=full displacement); Invert Weights as a frequently-used quick action.
- **Modifier stack order confirmed in frames:** Subdivision Surface (Catmull-Clark) above the Rock Displace modifier.

### Difficulty
Intermediate (two overlapping displacement systems with independent masks — easy to double-apply strength and mangle geometry if not careful, as the video itself demonstrates).

### Blender Version
Not stated verbally; viewport title bar in the captured frames is consistent with the same Blender 5.1.x build seen in other NS Rock Sculptor Guide episodes from this upload batch (2026-07-30). The Displace modifier itself is a long-standing core Blender modifier, version-independent.
