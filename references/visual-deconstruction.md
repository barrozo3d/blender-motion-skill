# Visual Deconstruction Framework

A systematic method for reading any 3D render and mapping it to Blender techniques.

---

## 1. Geometry Analysis

### Questions to ask first:
- Is the object hard-surface, organic, or a natural phenomenon?
- Is the geometry static or procedurally generated?
- Is there visible topology structure (wireframe traces, subdivision artifacts)?
- Does it look like it was modeled by hand or generated by algorithm?

### Indicators:
| What you see | Likely technique |
|---|---|
| Perfectly smooth curved surfaces | Subdivision Surface modifier |
| Repeated patterns with variation | Geometry Nodes + Instance on Points |
| Organic flowing forms | Sculpt mode or procedural noise displacement |
| Fractal/recursive detail | Recursive GeoNodes or displacement |
| Interconnected network/graph | Curves + Geometry Nodes |
| Growth/branching | L-system or GeoNodes clock-based spread |
| Fabric/textile detail | Curve-based knit or Cloth simulation |
| Architectural precision | Hard-surface modeling + boolean |

## 2. Material Analysis

### Metal
- **Polished gold/chrome**: low roughness (0.0–0.1), metallic=1.0, warm IOR
- **Brushed metal**: anisotropic shader, directional roughness
- **Oxidized/aged metal**: roughness map + color variation, subsurface hints
- **Liquid mercury**: metallic=1.0, roughness≈0, very high reflectivity

### Glass & Refractive
- **Clear glass**: transmission=1.0, IOR≈1.45, thin film interference
- **Frosted glass**: transmission + roughness 0.1–0.4
- **Liquid in glass**: volume absorption inside closed mesh
- **Caustics visible**: Cycles + caustics enabled, many samples

### Particles & Dust
- **Fine powder/dust**: tiny sphere instances with principled shader, density variation
- **Gold dust**: metallic sphere instances, roughness 0.05–0.2, warm color
- **Smoke-like diffuse**: volume scatter, density driven by simulation

### Organic/Biological
- **Skin/flesh**: subsurface scattering, radius RGB different per channel
- **Plant material**: translucency + subsurface, green SSS
- **Water droplets**: glass shader + volume + normal map on surface

### Emission
- **Glowing elements**: Emission shader, strength >1 for bloom
- **Neon**: emission + bloom in compositor
- **Bioluminescence**: mix of translucency + emission at low strength

## 3. Lighting & Ambiance

### Lighting Rig Types

#### Studio (most common for product/brand)
- 3-point: key light (area, high intensity), fill (area, soft, opposite), rim (behind subject)
- Key indicators: defined shadow on one side, bright catchlight in specular, clean background
- Blender setup: 3 Area lights, HDRI at low strength for ambient fill

#### HDRI Only
- Even ambient lighting from all directions
- Indicators: multiple soft shadows, environment reflections in specular
- Blender setup: World > Environment Texture node pointing to .hdr file
- Best HDRIs: Poly Haven (polyhaven.com) — free, high quality

#### Volume/Fog Atmosphere
- Indicators: light rays (god rays), depth haze, light scattering visible
- Blender setup: World volume scatter + Principled Volume shader on a cube
- Cycles only (EEVEE has volumetrics but lower quality)

#### Dramatic Single-Source
- Hard shadow, one dominant direction
- Indicators: crisp shadow edge, high contrast, specular highlight on one side only
- Blender setup: single Sun or Spot lamp, no fill

#### Emissive Environment
- Subject glows or background emits light
- Indicators: no visible light source, scene lit from within
- Blender setup: Emission shader on background geometry or World shader

### Ambiance Cues
| Visual cue | Blender approach |
|---|---|
| Warm golden hour feel | HDRI with warm tones + orange area light |
| Cold/clinical | White/blue area lights, low ambient |
| Dark luxury | Single rim light, black backdrop, high contrast |
| Cosmic/space | Black world, emissive particles, no fill light |
| Industrial | Mixed warm/cool, visible light sources in scene |
| Dreamy/ethereal | Volume scatter, soft bloom, desaturated with pastel accents |

### Light Color Temperature Reference (Blender 4.5+)
- 2700K — warm incandescent/candle
- 4000K — neutral white
- 5500K — daylight
- 6500K — cool daylight/overcast
- 8000K — blue sky shade

Blender 4.5 introduced the Temperature parameter on lights (Kelvin scale) — use it directly instead of manually tinting color.

## 4. Composition Analysis

### Camera
| What you see | Camera setting |
|---|---|
| Tight product shot, no distortion | Long focal length (85–135mm) |
| Wide environmental shot | Short focal length (24–35mm) |
| Dramatic perspective distortion | Ultra-wide (14–24mm) |
| Perfectly flat/orthographic | Orthographic camera |
| Tilt-shift / miniature look | Shift X/Y on camera + compositor DOF |

### Depth of Field
- Sharp subject, blurred background = aperture (f-stop) between f/1.4–f/4
- Everything sharp = f/8+
- Bokeh shape = aperture blades setting on camera
- Blender: Camera > Depth of Field > Aperture

### Composition Rules (identify which is used)
- **Rule of thirds**: subject at intersection points
- **Central symmetry**: subject dead center, usually for product heroes
- **Dutch angle**: camera slightly rotated — energy/tension
- **Low angle**: power, monumentality (camera below subject looking up)
- **Bird's eye**: overview, pattern-focused
- **Macro/extreme close-up**: material-as-subject, texture becomes landscape

### Motion Composition
- **Static camera, moving subject**: focus on transformation
- **Orbiting camera**: product reveal, 360° showcase
- **Push-in**: reveal, building tension toward climax
- **Pull-out**: context reveal, scale reveal
- **Parallax (multi-layer)**: depth illusion, multiple objects at different distances

## 5. Motion & Animation Analysis

### Motion Type
| What you see | Technique |
|---|---|
| Smooth organic motion | F-curve with bezier handles, ease in/out |
| Snappy/mechanical motion | Linear interpolation or stepped |
| Physically accurate bounce | Rigid body simulation |
| Procedural noise motion | GeoNodes noise texture on position |
| Looping seamlessly | NLA editor loop, or cycle modifier on f-curves |
| Freeze-frame then burst | Velocity-based motion blur + sudden movement |

### Motion Blur
- **Per-object blur** (fast object, sharp background): object motion blur, Cycles
- **Full scene blur**: camera shutter, render motion blur
- **Frozen/directional blur**: rendered at high shutter angle, composited
- **No blur, just fast**: high frame rate or ease on timing

### Timing & Rhythm
- Count the beat: does motion sync to audio? (common in Albin Merle's work)
- Is it a loop? Look for seamless start/end
- Is there a hold? (static moment before/after motion) — adds weight

## 6. Post-Processing Tells

### Blender Compositor
- **Bloom/glare**: Compositor > Glare node (Fog Glow or Ghosts)
- **Chromatic aberration**: Lens Distortion node, Distortion + Dispersion
- **Vignette**: Ellipse Mask + color correction
- **Color grading**: Color Balance node (Lift/Gamma/Gain)

### After Effects (Albin Merle pipeline)
- **Film grain**: AE > Add Grain effect or separate grain pass
- **Color grade on top**: Lumetri Color or Look panel
- **Lens flares**: optical flares plugin or AE native lens flare
- **Speed ramps**: Time Remapping on footage layer

### Render Engine Tell
| Visual quality | Engine |
|---|---|
| Perfect caustics, accurate GI | Cycles (high samples) |
| Fast, slight shading artifacts | EEVEE |
| Perfectly clean, no noise | Cycles + denoising |
| Grainy/filmic | Cycles at lower samples, intentional |
